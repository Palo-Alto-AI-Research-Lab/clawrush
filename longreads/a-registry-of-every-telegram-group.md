# A Registry of Every Telegram Group

There is another task in my plans, and I am about to get to it.

I need to build a registry of every Telegram group across all our accounts.

Each group has to be sub-classified: what happens in it, what is useful and what is not, who writes to us there, what we already have with those people, and where they might be leading us.

All of it has to be reconciled against our database.

We should have a list of groups where a person is present.

Groups split into two parts:
- Our groups, the ones we created.
- Other people's groups, where we are in some close circle.

If there is a company we work with or want to work with, we need to understand whether we have any groups in common with their employees. If such groups exist, we list those too.

In short, we need to prepare the cross-linking of everything with everything.

## What this actually is, and where the line runs

Underneath the tidy description this is a graph problem with a sharp edge on it.

**The useful output is a path, not a profile.** "Do we share a room with anyone at this company" is a routing question: it tells you whether an introduction exists or whether you are sending a cold message. That is ordinary business development and it has been done with address books for a century. The moment the same data is used to assemble what a specific person is into, reading their messages across rooms and building a picture of them, it stops being routing.

Our rule is the boundary, and it is written down: do not compile personal information across sources. A shared-group edge, yes. A dossier, no. In practice that means what gets stored is the edge and the room, not a portrait of the human on the other end.

**Identity resolution is the hard technical part, and it is where errors hurt people.** The same person shows up as a display name in one room, a handle in another, a phone contact in a third, and none of those are stable: handles get transferred, names get changed, and two strangers can share a name. Merging two humans into one record produces confident nonsense about both. The rule that holds: an identifier is not an identity, and a match that cannot be evidenced stays a candidate rather than a fact.

**Value has to be a verdict, not a vibe.** "What is useful here" ends as a state you can act on: worth reading regularly, worth reading during a specific project, or worth leaving. A registry where every row is marked interesting is a list of rooms.

**Rooms rot faster than people.** A group that was the centre of a topic in January is a graveyard in August. Anything in this registry carries the date of the last time it was actually useful, or the registry becomes a map of a city that has moved.
