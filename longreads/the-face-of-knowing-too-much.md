# The Face of Knowing Too Much

Fourth part of the thought about history and knowing. It follows directly from [the last one](the-more-i-learn-the-less-i-sleep.md), where the whole point was that the one who starts digging cannot sleep.

When I was walking through the archaeological museum in Madrid, looking at the sculptures of ancient thinkers, I thought that I am somewhat like them. Except that I am only forty-something, and these thinkers in the sculptures looked sixty or seventy. Judging by their faces, anyway.

I look at all this heritage of ancient civilizations and I understand that I understand nothing.

And if these people, the Aristotle types and the rest of them, really existed, and of course I do not know all their names, I still remember that the typical image of an ancient Greek thinker is always a very old man.

A face full of folds. A hooked nose of an irregular, unattractive shape. The forehead is unattractive too, the nose and the forehead both furrowed. And a beard. A ragged beard. Not a beautifully groomed beard, a genuinely ragged one.

So here is the thing. Maybe that is a pattern. A typical image that shows through when you know too much. When you have learned too much and understood that you understand nothing at all.
