# Six Subscriptions, One Question, and the Disagreement Is the Answer

Deep Research is not a cheap pleasure when you try to launch it through Claude Code. I worked that out back in June.

Back then the price came out somewhere between five dollars for the cheapest option, just rummaging around the internet, and fifty dollars if you wanted verification and checks on top.

In short, information became flatly equal to money.

You look something up on the internet, and that costs money. Any information on the internet costs money. Searching for that information costs money. Verifying it costs money too.

I used to think that now, with ChatGPT and with code, information would stop being valuable. That any person could know any information. And in a way that is true. But it turns out there is information, and then there is fact-checking, when all of it has been glued together, cross-checked, and so on. Those are two different products with two different prices.

Since June my whole approach changed. Paying for each piece of research separately is a dead end: you can burn money that way forever. So I no longer buy research by the unit. I fan the same question out across six subscriptions at once: ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, claude.ai, GLM and Mistral. Each one answers in its own way, and then I look at where they converged and where they split.

When six models answer one question, the divergence between them is the most valuable information you get. Where all six agree, you do not need to re-check. Where they disagree, that is exactly where you look with your own hands.

So the rule is simple: first squeeze what the subscription already paid for, and only then pay on top.

A note on how we run this in practice. The threshold we use is four rails out of six: a research task counts as done when any four independent rails have answered, and the threshold deliberately does not name which four. Yesterday the silent one was Gemini, today it is GLM. Naming specific vendors in the rule would turn one vendor's bad afternoon into a blocked pipeline. The rails that did not answer are not quietly forgotten either: they stay listed as missing, because a rail that failed does not repair itself just because the other four were enough.

And one more thing about the last few weeks with Claude Code. I have mostly not been coding. I have been building the environment: making sure Claude Code knows everything about me, and that all of it loads automatically.
